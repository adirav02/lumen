# Module 1 Examination

Time allowed: 60 minutes. Total: 100 points. A calculator and the supplied formula sheet are permitted. Show assumptions, units, and intermediate steps. Unless stated otherwise, values are exact synthetic teaching inputs. The general limitation of a model is not a reason to avoid a requested conditional calculation.

## Formula sheet

- 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ; 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg.
- MSJ-1990-S = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + s, with W in kg, H in cm, A in years; supplied branch constant s = +5 or −161.
- CUN-1980 = 500 + 22L; use F as a proxy for L only where explicitly authorized.
- FFM-370-21.6 = 370 + 21.6F; F = W(1 − p), with p a fraction.
- Gross activity energy = MET × 3.5 × W / 200 × minutes.
- Standard-rest-net activity energy = (MET − 1) × 3.5 × W / 200 × minutes.
- Conditional TDEE estimate = mean intake − k × weight-change slope.

## Short questions: 5 points each

**M01-Q01 — Units.** Convert 500 kcal to kJ. State whether kcal/day represents energy or a rate.

**M01-Q02 — Measurement.** Explain why a report saying only “BMR = 1,800” is insufficient to establish that a basal measurement took place. Identify two missing details.

**M01-Q03 — Inference.** A person's scale weight rises 0.8 kg overnight. Can you calculate exactly how much fat was gained? Explain.

**M01-Q04 — Model identity.** Why is “I used the Cunningham equation” an incomplete report? Specify the additional information needed for a reproducible calculation.

**M01-Q05 — Missing values.** In the complete tracking file, why is the blank intake on day 28 not an internally missing intake interval?

**M01-Q06 — Figures.** Does Figure M01-F01 establish that higher estimated body fat causes lower measured RMR in the same person? Distinguish a model relationship from a causal finding.

## Quantitative and analytical questions: 10 points each

**M01-Q07 — Three estimates.** Use CASE-ALEX-01: 80 kg, 180 cm, 25 years, published male MSJ branch, body fat 20%. Calculate F, MSJ-1990-S, CUN-1980 using F as a proxy, and FFM-370-21.6. Explain why their difference does not show a physiological change.

**M01-Q08 — Sensitivity.** In the same case, the estimated body fat changes from 20% to 23%, while all other inputs remain fixed. Calculate the change in F and in CUN-1980. State why this change is three percentage points rather than a 3% relative increase, and why the output range is not a 95% confidence interval.

**M01-Q09 — Gross and net.** WALK-B uses an 80 kg person, an assigned 4 MET, and 30 minutes. Calculate gross energy and standard-rest-net energy. If a whole-day model already includes resting expenditure, explain why gross activity energy cannot simply be added without checking overlap.

**M01-Q10 — Trend timing.** Mean weight for days 1–7 is 80.20 kg and for days 22–28 is 79.15. Calculate the slope using the separation of the window centers. Estimate TDEE with constant intake of 2,400 kcal/day and assumed k = 7,700 kcal/kg. Explain why a denominator of 28 is inappropriate for this particular comparison.

**M01-Q11 — Expenditure decline.** Baseline measured expenditure is 2,800 kcal/day. After weight loss, a reference model predicts 2,650; the later measured value is 2,550. Calculate the total observed decline, expected decline, and residual relative to the reference model. State one reason not to label the entire decline adaptive thermogenesis.

## Integrated critique: 20 points

**M01-Q12 — Audit a flawed report.** A trainee submits:

> “My predicted RMR is 1,805, so that is my measured TDEE. I multiply it by 1.6, which already includes my usual walking, and then add WALK-A's 252 gross calories. The first and fourth weekly averages fell by 1.05 kg over 28 days, proving my actual maintenance is exactly 2,688.75 kcal/day. I missed one intake entry, so I entered zero. My weight did not fall yesterday, so permanent metabolic damage is confirmed.”

Identify and repair **five distinct errors**. For each, state the error and a defensible alternative. Do not replace the report with a new personal diet prescription.
