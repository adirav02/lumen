# Module 5 source register

Verified 2026-10-22 where accessible. All templates, thresholds, cases, charts, and software policies are authored course material. They are not research participant data or uniquely optimal individual prescriptions. Citation titles identify original records; narrative guidance, intervention trials, meta-analyses, and practical conventions retain different roles.

## P01 — ACSM 2026 guidance and official publication summary

Currier BS et al.(2026). American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand: Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897. [Original record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/). [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/). [ACSM official summary, 17 March 2026](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/).

Role: broad resistance-training evidence and goal-specific prescription. Official summary accessible; direct PMC open returned a browser check in this verification. No exact simulator threshold is attributed to the position stand. The overview synthesizes reviews; it is not one new randomized training trial.

## P02 — Historical progression guidance

American College of Sports Medicine(2009). Progression models in resistance training for healthy adults. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 41(3):687–708. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/).

Role: historical load-progression context; abstract accessible. The dashboard's all-set/two-distinct-exposure rule, RIR criterion, and available-increment input are its own explicit teaching implementation, not a verbatim reproduction or universal optimum from this source.

## P03 — Volume-equated periodization synthesis

Moesgaard L, Beck MM, Christiansen L, Aagaard P, Lundbye-Jensen J(2022). Effects of Periodization on Strength and Muscle Hypertrophy in Volume-Equated Resistance Training Programs: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Sports Medicine 52:1647–1666. DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01636-1. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/).

Role: distinguish 1RM-strength and hypertrophy results in volume-equated comparisons. Abstract accessible. A strength advantage does not establish a universal hypertrophy advantage or validate this module's six-week set sequence.

## P04 — Original cessation/deload intervention

Coleman M et al.(2024). Gaining more from doing less? The effects of a one-week deload period during supervised resistance training on muscular adaptations. PeerJ12:e16777. DOI 10.7717/peerj.16777. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/). [Full-text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/).

Role: the specific week-without-RT intervention, with outcome-specific interpretation. Abstract accessible: no appreciable lower-body hypertrophy/endurance/power advantage; continuous condition favored lower-body strength. Do not generalize this implementation to all reduced-load/volume deloads.

## P05 — Newer deload record, further reading with access limitation

Pancar Z et al.(2026). Effects of deload periods in resistance training on muscle hypertrophy and strength endurance in untrained young men using a randomized within subject design. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41730991/). [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13031491/).

Role: explicitly separate newer volume/frequency-reduction research from P04's cessation protocol. Search record accessible; direct PubMed content was incomplete and PMC returned a browser check. No detailed result or effect size from this record is used in the lessons, calculations, or gold answers. Read the full verified record before adding such claims.

## P06 — Concurrent training synthesis

Schumann M et al.(2022; online 2021). Compatibility of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training for Skeletal Muscle Size and Function: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/). [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/).

Role: distinguish pooled hypertrophy/maximal-strength findings from explosive-strength vulnerability and scheduling context. Original abstract/full-text search record accessible; a later direct PubMed request returned 429. Results do not provide a universal interval guaranteeing zero interference.

## P07 — Bodybuilding-specific training review

Helms ER, Fitschen PJ, Aragon AA, Cronin J, Schoenfeld BJ(2015 issue;2014 online). Recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: resistance and cardiovascular training. Journal of Sports Medicine and Physical Fitness 55(3):164–178. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24998610/).

Role: context for resistance/aerobic planning during contest preparation. Original record accessible. Contest preparation is not a universal template for general fitness. Online/issue dates refer to the same work, not two trials.

## P08 — Athlete sleep consensus

Walsh NP et al. Sleep and the athlete: narrative review and 2021 expert consensus recommendations. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102025. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33144349/). [Journal](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/7/356).

Role: individualized sleep considerations and distinction between opportunity and obtained sleep. PubMed abstract accessible; journal page returned 403. Does not supply an exact muscle-growth percentage per hour or a one-night set-reduction formula.

## Integration foundations from earlier modules

P09: Mifflin MD et al.(1990). A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. [Original PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/). Equation and branch definitions are taught in HN-M01-L02 and explicitly repeated for CAP-A. Prediction is not direct calorimetry.

P10: Aragon AA et al.(2017). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: diets and body composition. [Full-text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5470183/). Earlier Modules 1–2 supply the energy/body-composition foundations. The dashboard's−10% scenario and static 7700 coefficient are stated teaching choices, not universal individual prescriptions from this source.

P11: Jäger R et al.(2017). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. [Full-text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/). Module 2 provides context for protein ranges and measurement denominators. The selected 1.8 g/kg BW case factor is an input, not an individualized measured requirement or guaranteed synthesis threshold.

P12: Iraki J, Fitschen P, Espinar S, Helms E(2019). Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season: A Narrative Review. [Full-text record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/). Earlier Module 2 treats energy-surplus scenarios and uncertainty. This integration lesson does not convert a surplus percentage into an exact rate of new muscle gain.

P09–P12 are retained foundation links with prior module teaching context; they were not independently revalidated in full during this module's source check. The coefficient identities 500+22×lean-mass input and 370+21.6×FFM retain the naming/measurement caveats documented in Module 1.

## Authored conventions

The two-exposure progression policy, six-week offsets and ceiling, template set counts, proposed RIR targets, calculator input domains, mastery cutoff, arbitrary-unit classroom calculations, and evaluator scores are transparent teaching conventions. They are not validated medical instruments, exact recovery laws, or independent platform performance results.
