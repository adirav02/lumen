# HN-M04-EX01 — Biomechanics and Exercise Execution

**Time:** 90 minutes. **Total:** 100 points. **Materials:** calculator and the supplied figures; attempt closed book first. All numeric examples are synthetic. A scientifically justified statement of insufficient information can earn full credit where appropriate. No loaded physical test is required.

Questions 1–12 carry 5 points each. Questions 13–16 carry 10 points each. Show units and distinguish a calculated external component from a measured internal quantity. A course mastery threshold of 75/100 is a teaching convention, not an externally accredited qualification.

## Short questions — 60 points

1. **Force and mass.** Calculate the weight assigned to a 12 kg object using g=9.81 m/s². Is that result the object's mass? Explain the units.

2. **Figure reading.** Inspect assets/M04-F01_moment_arm.png. Calculate the moment magnitude about the pivot. Explain why the radius label is not the correct perpendicular distance.

3. **Angles.** A 100 N force acts at a radius of 0.30 m. The angle between radius and force is 30°. Calculate the perpendicular distance and moment magnitude. State what angle was used.

4. **Quantity identity.** A 3×10×80 kg log has volume-load 2400 kg. Does it establish mechanical work in joules or a shoulder moment in N·m? Name what is missing.

5. **Range of motion.** With 0° at full knee extension, compare angular spans 20°–100° and 60°–100°. Does their ratio establish the ratio of expected hypertrophy?

6. **Squat judgment.** Two lifters use different torso inclinations at the same stated depth. Is femur length alone sufficient to call one incorrect? Give two other relevant setup or body variables.

7. **Two-joint anatomy.** At a fixed knee position, what is the directional effect of reducing hip flexion from 90° to 40° on rectus-femoris length across the hip? Which hip-angle convention is required?

8. **Hinge calculation.** Calculate HINGE-B using bar mass 40 kg, g=9.81 m/s², and horizontal hip-to-bar-force-line distance 0.25 m. Does it identify lumbar compression?

9. **Hamstrings.** Name the hamstring that does not cross the hip. Explain why a hip thrust is not a task-matched replacement for a knee curl.

10. **Press cues.** Explain why a stable retracted bench setup must not automatically become a rule to pin the scapula down and back throughout overhead arm elevation.

11. **Pull directions.** Distinguish scapular retraction from humeral horizontal abduction. Does a wider grip alone prove greater future lat hypertrophy?

12. **Calf evidence.** The standing/seated comparison discussed in this module favored standing for gastrocnemius, with similar soleus changes. Evaluate the claim: “The study proves standing produced more growth in every calf muscle.”

## Applied cases — 40 points

13. **Resistance-profile audit.** PROFILE-A uses force 100 N with perpendicular distances 0.10, 0.30, 0.15 m. PROFILE-B uses 100 N with distances 0.25, 0.20, 0.05 m. Calculate all six moments, identify the larger demand at each named position, and explain two things these samples cannot establish. In particular, address whether “start” means the target is lengthened.

14. **Observation versus diagnosis.** A log says: “Leg press 100 kg, painful at bottom; knees forward; therefore ankle injury.” Rewrite it as a useful observation record. Include at least four setup/behavior fields, one appropriate immediate task decision, and two conclusions the supplied information cannot justify. Explain why an ideal 100 kg frictionless 45° sled calculation does not automatically describe this machine.

15. **Substitution report.** An overhead cable triceps station is occupied. Available equipment includes dumbbells and a bench; an arm-at-side cable pressdown is also available elsewhere. Compare an overhead dumbbell extension and a pressdown. State the action preserved by both, the secondary-joint feature each preserves or changes, why exact resistance-profile equivalence is not established, and a sensible fresh baseline. Do not certify suitability for symptoms.

16. **Integrated geometry and uncertainty.** A behind-body curl supplies 80 N cable tension at a starting perpendicular elbow moment arm of 0.05 m. Another behind-body curl supplies the same tension at 0.20 m. Calculate the starting moments and ratio. Then evaluate: “Both are in a long-muscle position, so they must have identical loading and growth.” State two additional observations needed for a useful comparison and one outcome that still requires longitudinal measurement.

Submit calculations, reasoning, and assumptions. Instructor key is distributed separately.
